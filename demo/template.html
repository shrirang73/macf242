Why not just buy NIFTY?

Buying the index earns more in a rising market but rides every crash. The hedged desk earns a smaller premium that barely depends on market direction. Same capital, same period:

Where the maths enters

φ = ∂C/∂F + (ρ·ξ / F) · ∂C/∂v

The first term is the ordinary delta with volatility held fixed. The second term hedges the part of the volatility move that comes with the index move: NIFTY falls when volatility jumps (ρ < 0), so the desk holds slightly fewer futures. ρ and ξ come from fitting the Heston model to that day's option prices.

What can go wrong

The desk earns a little on most days and loses a lot on a few. Costs decide whether anything is left.

The worst stretches

  • Short options lose fast in crashes; leverage multiplies those losses as much as the gains.
  • Costs are assumptions (futures fee per trade and half the option bid–ask spread); our data has no bid–ask quotes.
  • Margin, funding and exchange rules are not modelled. Past results do not predict future ones.
